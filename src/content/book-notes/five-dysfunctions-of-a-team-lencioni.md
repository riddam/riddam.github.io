In today's fast-paced corporate world, teamwork is the backbone of success — yet even the most talented teams falter when underlying dysfunctions go unaddressed. Patrick Lencioni's *The Five Dysfunctions of a Team* examines why teams struggle and offers a practical model for building a high-performing one. These notes walk through each dysfunction with a real-world example of its impact.

*Originally published [on Medium](https://medium.com/@riddamjain/the-five-dysfunctions-of-a-team-by-patrick-lencioni-book-summary-eacd87e44b92) in March 2025, lightly edited here.*

The five dysfunctions build on each other like a pyramid — each one rests on the one below, so trust is the foundation and inattention to results sits at the top.

## 1. Absence of Trust

Trust is the foundation of any strong team. Without it, members hesitate to be vulnerable, admit mistakes, or ask for help — and collaboration stalls.

**Real-life example:** A tech startup struggled because engineers were afraid to admit when they didn't understand parts of the codebase. Instead of asking for help, they burned hours figuring things out alone, slowing delivery.

**Fix:** Encourage vulnerability — share personal experiences, run trust-building exercises, and treat mistakes as learning opportunities rather than failures.

> "Trust is knowing that when a team member pushes you, they are doing it because they care about the team."

## 2. Fear of Conflict

When teams avoid difficult conversations, they never address what's actually blocking progress. Healthy debate drives innovation; fear of conflict produces artificial harmony.

**Real-life example:** In a large enterprise, managers avoided confronting an underperformer. Rather than discuss it openly, they quietly reassigned tasks to others — breeding resentment and a heavier load for the high performers.

**Fix:** Promote open dialogue, establish psychological safety, and encourage constructive criticism before issues escalate.

> "If we don't trust one another, then we aren't going to engage in open, constructive, ideological conflict."

## 3. Lack of Commitment

Without clear decisions, teams get stuck in ambiguity and second-guessing, which leads to delays and inefficiency.

**Real-life example:** A product team couldn't settle on a core feature because discussion never ended. With no consensus, progress stalled and market opportunities slipped away.

**Fix:** Create clarity — set deadlines for decisions, make sure everyone is heard, and commit to a plan even when not everyone agrees 100%.

> "A team that commits… creates clarity around direction and priorities."

## 4. Avoidance of Accountability

Holding each other accountable keeps a team on track. But many teams hesitate to challenge peers when standards slip.

**Real-life example:** On a sales team, one rep consistently missed targets. Teammates noticed but said nothing — and overall performance suffered.

**Fix:** Set clear goals, review progress regularly, and build a culture where peer-to-peer accountability is normal.

> "The enemy of accountability is ambiguity."

## 5. Inattention to Results

When people prioritize personal success over collective goals, the team's results suffer.

**Real-life example:** A marketing team optimized for individual KPIs (personal leads) rather than the company's goal of brand growth. Short-term wins, long-term stagnation.

**Fix:** Align team objectives with company goals, celebrate team wins, and measure success collectively rather than individually.

> "Great teams do not hold back with one another. They are unafraid to air their dirty laundry."

## Conclusion

The Five Dysfunctions framework is a powerful lens for diagnosing and resolving team problems. Build trust, embrace conflict, commit to decisions, hold each other accountable, and focus on results — and a team can reach its full potential.
